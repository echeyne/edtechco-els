\begin{abstract}
Every US state publishes early learning standards --- the developmental
expectations that shape curriculum and assessment from birth to kindergarten ---
and more than fifty jurisdictions each design their own document. The result is
public policy that software cannot read: multi-column tables, age-banded grids
and prose narratives whose typography is shared by no two states. The obstacle
is not text recovery but hierarchy recovery, because a standard is meaningful
only through its ancestry, and the surface cues that would reveal that ancestry
are exactly what varies. Arizona writes \texttt{Strand 1:}, Kentucky writes
\texttt{Benchmark 1.1}, Nevada's printed code namespace skips a level, and
Colorado publishes no sub-strand tier at all.

We present an LLM-driven method that classifies each element by its
\emph{nesting position} rather than its label vocabulary. A first pass infers a
per-document depth map --- how many levels this document uses and what each
looks like in it --- and a second classifies every element against that map as
authority; a decoupled parsing stage then resolves the flat result into one
canonical schema with deterministic, collision-free identifiers, and a
serverless batching architecture applies it to whole documents. The system
contains no per-state rule.

Evaluated on four development states and two held out entirely, detector recall
is 1.000 at every hierarchy level in all six, and a manual audit of all 296
in-scope detections finds a single hallucinated element. Ablating the depth-map
pass leaves domain and indicator recall untouched while strand falls to 0.920
and sub-strand to 0.833 --- degrading exactly the levels whose identity is
positional --- and a rule-based extractor matches the method at a document's top
division while collapsing beneath it. Repeated runs show the pipeline is not
deterministic: instability is concentrated in three known places, and one
previously recorded result of our own failed to reproduce, which we report along
with what it implies for single-run evidence. All quality figures are computed
on an annotated subset tier, stated as such throughout.
\end{abstract}
